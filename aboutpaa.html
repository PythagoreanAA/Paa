The method

What is
PAA?

Pythagorean Arithmosophic Anamnesis — PAA for short — is a way of reading the pattern underneath a person, a relationship, or a project, using eleven number-shapes as a map.

The idea

Number as a shape for behavior, not a decoration.

Most things that grow, change, or come apart — a person, a relationship, a company, an idea — pass through a similar sequence of phases: something begins, meets tension, finds a working shape, stabilizes, gets tested, and either renews or collapses.

PAA gives that sequence eleven names, one through nine plus eleven, and treats each one as a distinct move — a way of acting on a situation — rather than a lucky or unlucky number.

The claim is modest: these eleven moves recur often enough, across enough different situations, to be worth learning to recognize in your own life.

The sequence

Eleven recurring moves.

This isn't a fixed clock that ticks from one to the next. It's a set of moves to watch for — in yourself, in a conversation, in a project that's stuck.

1
Begin
The first move. Someone decides, and something starts.
2
Relate
Two things meet. Tension, comparison, and connection appear.
3
Express
The tension finds words, image, or shape — it becomes something that can be shared.
4
Build
Structure sets in: boundaries, roles, a foundation that can hold weight.
5
Change
Something tests the structure — variation, risk, a need to move.
6
Repair
Fragments get gathered back into balance. Care and responsibility.
7
Question
A crisis or a doubt breaks the surface. Something has to be examined honestly.
8
Push
Force, scale, and consequence. What was understood now has to produce a result.
9
Finish
Completion — sometimes satisfying, sometimes just exhaustion. A cycle ends.
11
Overflow
The finished cycle becomes aware of itself and starts again at a higher pitch — more intense, less contained by what came before.
Where it shows up

The same sequence, different scale.

A life
Birth, identity, experimenting, settling in, a crisis, and starting over.
A team
Founding, friction, finding a process, innovating, breaking, and reforming.
A society
Emerging, consolidating, expanding, decaying, collapsing, and rebuilding.
An idea
Discovery, argument, becoming accepted, hardening, being challenged, and changing.
A living thing
Variation, selection, settling in, mutation, dying out, and something new appearing.
Once you can see the shape, you stop needing a name for it.

Once these eleven moves are familiar, they stop feeling like abstract symbols and start working like a set of tools — a way of noticing a pattern in yourself, understanding a relationship, or making sense of why a project, a team, or a whole era of history keeps repeating itself.

See these moves as shapes, not just words.
